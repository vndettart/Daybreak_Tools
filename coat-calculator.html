📖 Gene key

O / o — orange vs. black (X-linked, so tortoiseshell only happens in females)
D / d — dense color vs. dilute (black→blue, orange→cream)
A / a — agouti (tabby) vs. solid
Ta / Mc / mc — ticked > mackerel > classic tabby pattern (only visible when tabby)
S / s — white spotting vs. none
Sv / Sh / Sm / Sl — spotting grade when S_: van > high (harlequin) > medium (bicolor) > low (mitted) — how much white a spotted cat shows
W / w — dominant white, masks every other gene
L / l — short coat vs. long coat
B / b / b° — black vs. chocolate vs. cinnamon (only visible in black-based fur)
C / cb / cs / c — full color > sepia (Burmese) > colorpoint (Siamese) > albino. cb+cs together = mink (Tonkinese)
I / i — silver/smoke inhibitor vs. none (pales the background on tabbies, frosts the undercoat on solids)
Wb / Wm / wb — wideband/tipping when I_: light (chinchilla, barely tipped) > medium (shaded) > heavy (full smoke/silver tabby) — how much of each hair carries color
Spotting grade and tipping are polygenic in real cats, not single clean genes like the others above — they're modeled here the same simplified way as Brown/Tabby-pattern/C-locus, as ordered tiers rather than a true blend. Eye color is handled separately still: colorpoint/mink cats are always blue-eyed and albinos are always pink-eyed, but otherwise it's a weighted random pick that (like real cats) leans toward blue and odd-eyed the more white a kitten has.
